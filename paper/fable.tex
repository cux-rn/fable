\begin{abstract}
We present \Fable, a family of symmetric primitives built on a single new component: \FableP, a 512-bit ARX permutation whose round is the \ChaCha double round with round constants injected into two state words. On top of \FableP we define a keyed-duplex authenticated cipher (\FableAEAD; 256-bit key, 192-bit nonce, 256-bit tag, rate $r=256$, capacity $c=256$), a chunked large-file mode (\FableStream, the STREAM construction with 64\,KiB chunks) and a sponge hash/XOF. Two parameter sets share everything but the number of data-phase rounds: \Fable uses $\PP{12}$ for initialisation/finalisation and $\PP{8}$ per data block, \FableF uses $\PP{12}/\PP{6}$; the parameter set is bound into the initial state, so the two are domain-separated.

The design goals are (i) nonces long enough to be chosen at random, (ii) key commitment without an extra pass, obtained by taking the tag directly from the truncated output of the finalising permutation, (iii) authentication in the same pass as encryption, and (iv) chunk-level parallelism that maps eight independent chunks onto AVX2 lanes.

We report a first-party analysis that is fully reproducible from the accompanying repository: avalanche statistics with $5\times10^5$ samples per round count; SAT-based (Lipmaa--Moriai) differential trail bounds, including the observation that every rotation-constant choice admits a probability-one differential through one quarter-round, and a rate-restricted model of the duplex data phase that yields a split bound of $2^{-\BoundEight}$ on any forgery trail through $\PP{8}$; a differential-linear evaluation calibrated against the \ChaCha literature (best key recovery: 7.5 single rounds), giving empirical margins of $2.1\times$ for the \Fable data phase and $3.2\times$ for initialisation; linear-trail bounds (exact for one round, a SAT lower bound for two) and a linear-hull enumeration; rotational-XOR, slide and fixed-point checks of the round constants; a key-commitment reduction (commitment strength $\tau/2$ bits for a $\tau$-bit tag); and multi-user bounds for the STREAM composition. An AVX2 implementation encrypts 1\,GiB at 2.00\,GB/s (2.44 cycles/byte) single-threaded for \Fable and 2.53\,GB/s for \FableF, on par with or faster than OpenSSL's ChaCha20-Poly1305 on the same machine, and scales to 11.5\,GB/s on eight cores. \Fable is a research draft: it has received no third-party analysis and must not be used to protect real data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Authenticated encryption in deployed protocols is dominated by two families: AES-GCM, which depends on hardware support for the block cipher and the field multiplication, and ChaCha20-Poly1305~\cite{RFC8439}, which runs on any CPU but pays for authentication with a second pass (Poly1305) over the data and inherits a 96-bit nonce that cannot safely be chosen at random---the extended-nonce variant XChaCha20-Poly1305~\cite{XChaCha} repairs the latter with an extra HChaCha20 call. Neither is key-committing~\cite{BH22}: for both, an adversary can craft a single ciphertext that decrypts correctly under two different keys, a property that has led to concrete attacks on message franking, envelope encryption and abuse-reporting systems. Permutation-based designs such as Ascon~\cite{Ascon21} and NORX~\cite{NORX14} solve the ``single pass'' part of the problem with the keyed duplex~\cite{BDPV11}, but they were optimised for lightweight or 64-bit-word platforms respectively, and the commitment question was not a design goal.

\Fable is an attempt to combine, in one primitive, the properties we believe a general-purpose file and stream encryption tool needs today:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A nonce that can be random.} \FableAEAD takes a 192-bit nonce. With a 152-bit random file nonce, \FableStream stays below a $2^{-73}$ collision probability for $2^{40}$ files under one key, and the caller may alternatively use a structured (counter) nonce with zero collision term (\S\ref{sec:stream}).
\item \textbf{Key commitment for free.} The tag is the 256-bit rate part of $\PP{12}(X)$, where $X$ is the complete internal state before finalisation---a state that contains the key (injected into the capacity), the nonce, the associated data, the message and the parameter-set identifier. We prove (\S\ref{sec:commitment}), in the ideal-permutation model, that any CMT-4 attack reduces to one of three events: a collision on the $\tau$-bit truncation of $\PP{12}$ (probability $\leq q^2/2^{\tau+1}$ after $q$ permutation queries), two executions under different keys reaching the same 512-bit state ($\leq q^2/2^{513}$), or a guess of the 256-bit capacity ($\leq q/2^{256}$); the last two are negligible, so a $\tau$-bit tag commits with $\tau/2$ bits of security---128 bits for the default 256-bit tag. This is the CTX idea of Chan and Rogaway~\cite{CR22} (commit through a hash of key, nonce, associated data and tag) realised inside the duplex, where the finalising permutation already plays the role of the hash.
\item \textbf{Authentication in the same pass.} \FableAEAD is a keyed duplex with $r=c=256$: every 32-byte block costs one call of the data-phase permutation and nothing else. There is no second pass, no polynomial MAC, and the mode inherits the multi-user bounds of the keyed duplex~\cite{JLM14,DMV17} (\S\ref{sec:mu}).
\item \textbf{Chunk-level parallelism.} \FableStream applies the STREAM construction~\cite{HRRV15} to 64\,KiB chunks. Chunks are independent AEAD instances, so eight of them are processed simultaneously in the eight 32-bit lanes of an AVX2 register file, with the sixteen state words kept in transposed layout for the whole AEAD; decryption is online and truncation, reordering and cross-file splicing are rejected by the STREAM reduction.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why a new permutation.}
The permutation \FableP is deliberately conservative: its round function is exactly the \ChaCha double round (column step followed by diagonal step, quarter-round rotations $(16,12,8,7)$)~\cite{Bernstein08chacha}, so that nearly two decades of differential-linear cryptanalysis of \ChaCha~\cite{AFKMR08,BLT20,BBCDFLNT22,CS21,DGSS22,DGM23,WLHL23,Dey24,SDSM25,FT25,FT25b} transfers, subject to the calibration in \S\ref{sec:security}. The one structural change is required by the change of use: \ChaCha is a PRF whose fixed input constants break symmetry, whereas a sponge permutation receives attacker-influenced input in every word and must break symmetry \emph{inside} the round. \FableP therefore XORs a round constant into two words, $S[0]$ and $S[5]$, chosen so that they lie in different columns, different rows and on the same diagonal. We show in \S\ref{sec:constants} that without the constants the ``four identical columns'' subspace of $2^{128}$ states is invariant under every round, and that the constants leave that subspace in every one of the twelve rounds.

\paragraph{Security argument.}
ARX permutations have no provable active-S-box bound; \FableP, like \ChaCha, even has probability-one differentials through a single quarter-round (for \emph{every} choice of rotation constants, \S\ref{sec:diff}). We therefore make no ``no characteristic above $2^{-128}$ in $n$ rounds'' claim. Instead the round counts are set by an empirical margin against the strongest published attack family on the same round function: with key recovery on 7.5 \ChaCha single rounds as the yardstick, the \Fable data phase ($\PP{8}$ = 16 single rounds) has a $2.1\times$ margin, initialisation ($\PP{12}$ = 24 single rounds) $3.2\times$, and \FableF ($\PP{6}$ = 12 single rounds) $1.6\times$, the margin of ChaCha12 as used in Adiantum~\cite{Adiantum18}. ChaCha20's own round count was fixed by Bernstein at design time~\cite{Bernstein08salsa,Bernstein08chacha}; the question of how much margin is enough has since been debated explicitly~\cite{Aumasson20}, and we make our choice and its yardstick visible rather than settle that debate. Where exact statements are possible we make them: SAT-proven trail bounds, an exact key-commitment reduction, and mode-level bounds in the ideal-permutation model.

\paragraph{Relation to existing constructions.}
Each of the four goals above is met by some deployed or standardised scheme; the point of \Fable is to meet all four with one permutation and one pass. XChaCha20-Poly1305~\cite{XChaCha} provides a 192-bit nonce and portable performance, and becomes key-committing when wrapped with CTX~\cite{CR22} at the cost of an additional hash of the key, nonce, associated data and tag; authentication remains a second pass, and there is no built-in chunked mode. AES-GCM-SIV~\cite{RFC8452} offers nonce-misuse resistance, which \Fable does not claim, but its nonce is 96 bits, it is not key-committing, and its speed depends on AES and carry-less multiplication hardware. Ascon-128a~\cite{Ascon21} is a keyed duplex with single-pass authentication and a 128-bit key; it targets constrained platforms, its nonce is 128 bits, and its tag is XORed with the key at finalisation, which is not the direct truncated-permutation output that our commitment reduction relies on. AEGIS~\cite{AEGIS13,AEGISdraft} reaches very high throughput with AES round instructions and has a 256-bit-nonce variant; it is not designed for key commitment, and it relies on hardware AES. \Fable sits in the software-only, ARX corner of this space: a random-safe nonce, single-pass authentication, commitment from the tag itself, and chunk-level SIMD parallelism, at the price of a permutation that---unlike AES or ChaCha20---has not yet received third-party analysis.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The specification of \FableP, \FableAEAD, \FableStream and \FableHash with two domain-separated parameter sets and test vectors (\S\ref{sec:spec}).
\item A design rationale that records, for each choice, the experiment that motivated it (\S\ref{sec:rationale}).
\item A reproducible first-party analysis (\S\ref{sec:security}): avalanche with $5\times10^5$ states per round count; SAT differential bounds in the unconstrained model (1 round: $2^{-2}$ exact; 2 rounds: $\leq 2^{-\WuncTwo}$) and in the rate-restricted duplex model (1 round: $2^{-22}$ exact; 2 rounds: $\leq 2^{-\WrateTwo}$; no internal collision within half a round), combined into a split bound of $2^{-\BoundEight}$ for any forgery trail through $\PP{8}$ and $2^{-\BoundSix}$ through $\PP{6}$; a rotation-constant screening over all $32^4$ choices for the single-quarter-round metric and 254,576 choices for the one-round metric, showing that neither metric can separate them; a differential-linear evaluation with $2^{22}$ samples per bit pair showing that the round constants do not change the bias envelope relative to the \ChaCha round structure; linear-trail bounds (1 round $2^{-1}$, exact; 2 rounds $\leq 2^{-\WlinTwo}$, SAT lower bound) and a hull enumeration (no hull effect in 1 round, a factor $\approx 9$ in 2 rounds); rotational-XOR bounds ($\leq 2^{-419}$ for $\PP{6}$); slide and fixed-point checks; the key-commitment reduction; and multi-user STREAM bounds.
\item Portable C and AVX2 implementations, byte-exact against the Python reference, with throughput measurements against OpenSSL on the same machine (\S\ref{sec:impl}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Status.}
\Fable is a research draft (specification v0.4). Every number in this paper is taken from the analysis log of the public repository, each with the command that regenerates it and the raw data file it was computed from; the log also lists what has \emph{not} been done (\S\ref{sec:conclusion}). Until independent cryptanalysis has been published, \Fable must not be used to protect real data.

\section{Specification}
\label{sec:spec}

This section is a transcription of specification v0.4 (\texttt{Fable-spec-v0.4.md} in the repository); the Python reference \texttt{fable.py} and the C implementation are the normative tie-breakers and agree bit for bit on all test vectors.

\subsection{Notation}
All words are 32-bit unsigned integers; byte strings are converted to words little-endian. $+$ denotes addition modulo $2^{32}$, $\oplus$ XOR, and $x \rotl n$ rotation of a 32-bit word to the left by $n$ positions. The state $S$ consists of sixteen words $S[0..15]$, viewed as a $4\times4$ matrix in row-major order:
\[
\begin{array}{cccc}
S[0] & S[1] & S[2] & S[3]\\
S[4] & S[5] & S[6] & S[7]\\
S[8] & S[9] & S[10] & S[11]\\
S[12] & S[13] & S[14] & S[15]
\end{array}
\]
The \emph{rate} is $R = S[0..7]$ (256 bits) and the \emph{capacity} is $C = S[8..15]$ (256 bits). $\PP{n}$ denotes $n$ rounds of \FableP. $\mathrm{pad}(x)$ is the $10^*$ padding of a byte string $x$ with $|x| < 32$ bytes to 32 bytes: append the byte \texttt{0x80}, then zero bytes.

\subsection{The permutation \FableP}
\label{sec:perm}

\paragraph{Quarter-round.} $Q(a,b,c,d)$ updates four words in place with the rotation constants $(r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3) = (16,12,8,7)$:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
a \mathrel{+}= b;\; d \mathrel{\oplus}= a;\; d \mathrel{\rotl}= r_0; &
c \mathrel{+}= d;\; b \mathrel{\oplus}= c;\; b \mathrel{\rotl}= r_1;\\
a \mathrel{+}= b;\; d \mathrel{\oplus}= a;\; d \mathrel{\rotl}= r_2; &
c \mathrel{+}= d;\; b \mathrel{\oplus}= c;\; b \mathrel{\rotl}= r_3.
\end{array}
\]
This is the \ChaCha quarter-round.

\paragraph{Round.} Round $i$ ($0 \le i \le 11$) consists of constant injection, a column step and a diagonal step:
\begin{align*}
&S[0] \mathrel{\oplus}= \mathrm{RC}[i];\qquad S[5] \mathrel{\oplus}= \mathrm{RC}[i] \rotl 16;\\
&Q(S[0],S[4],S[8],S[12]);\quad Q(S[1],S[5],S[9],S[13]);\\
&Q(S[2],S[6],S[10],S[14]);\quad Q(S[3],S[7],S[11],S[15]);\\
&Q(S[0],S[5],S[10],S[15]);\quad Q(S[1],S[6],S[11],S[12]);\\
&Q(S[2],S[7],S[8],S[13]);\quad Q(S[3],S[4],S[9],S[14]).
\end{align*}
The column and diagonal steps together are the \ChaCha double round.

\paragraph{Round constants.} $\mathrm{RC}[i]$ is the first 32 bits of the fractional part of $\ln p_i$, where $p_i$ is the $(i{+}1)$-th prime ($2, 3, 5, \ldots, 37$); the twelve values are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:rc}. $\PP{12}$ uses $\mathrm{RC}[0..11]$ in order. A reduced permutation $\PP{n}$ with $n<12$ uses the \emph{last} $n$ constants, $\mathrm{RC}[12-n..11]$: $\PP{8}$ uses $\mathrm{RC}[4..11]$ and $\PP{6}$ uses $\mathrm{RC}[6..11]$, so that the light permutation is a suffix of the heavy one.

\subsection{\FableAEAD}
\label{sec:aead}
Inputs: key $K$ (256 bits, words $K[0..7]$), nonce $N$ (192 bits, words $N[0..5]$), associated data $A$ and message $M$ of arbitrary byte length (a single message is limited to $2^{64}$ bytes; larger objects use \FableStream). Output: ciphertext $C$ with $|C| = |M|$ and a 256-bit tag $T$; $T$ may be truncated by the application to no fewer than 128 bits. $\mathrm{IV}_0 = \texttt{0x4661626C}$ (ASCII ``Fabl'').

\begin{algorithm}[htb]
\caption{\FableAEAD encryption, parameter set with data-phase rounds $d$ and constant $\mathrm{IV}_1$}
\label{alg:aead}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $K \in \{0,1\}^{256}$, $N \in \{0,1\}^{192}$, $A, M \in \{0,1\}^{8*}$
\Ensure $C \in \{0,1\}^{|M|}$, $T \in \{0,1\}^{256}$
\State $S \gets \mathrm{IV}_0 \,\|\, \mathrm{IV}_1 \,\|\, K[0..7] \,\|\, N[0..5]$ \Comment{$S[0]{=}\mathrm{IV}_0$, $S[1]{=}\mathrm{IV}_1$, $S[2..9]{=}K$, $S[10..15]{=}N$}
\State $S \gets \PP{12}(S)$
\State $S[8..15] \gets S[8..15] \oplus K[0..7]$ \Comment{key re-injected into the capacity}
\If{$|A| > 0$}
  \State split $A$ into 32-byte blocks $A_0,\ldots,A_{a-1}$ with $|A_{a-1}| < 32$ (the last block may be empty)
  \For{$j = 0$ \textbf{to} $a-2$}
    \State $S[0..7] \gets S[0..7] \oplus A_j$;\quad $S \gets \PP{d}(S)$
  \EndFor
  \State $S[0..7] \gets S[0..7] \oplus \mathrm{pad}(A_{a-1})$;\quad $S \gets \PP{d}(S)$
\EndIf
\State $S[15] \gets S[15] \oplus \texttt{0x80000000}$ \Comment{domain separation: end of associated data}
\State split $M$ into 32-byte blocks $M_0,\ldots,M_{m-1}$ with $|M_{m-1}| < 32$ (the last block may be empty)
\For{$j = 0$ \textbf{to} $m-2$}
  \State $S[0..7] \gets S[0..7] \oplus M_j$;\quad $C_j \gets S[0..7]$;\quad $S \gets \PP{d}(S)$
\EndFor
\State $S[0..7] \gets S[0..7] \oplus \mathrm{pad}(M_{m-1})$;\quad $C_{m-1} \gets$ first $|M_{m-1}|$ bytes of $S[0..7]$
\State $S[8..15] \gets S[8..15] \oplus K[0..7]$ \Comment{finalisation}
\State $S \gets \PP{12}(S)$
\State $T \gets S[0..7]$ \Comment{the tag is the rate itself; it is \emph{not} XORed with the key}
\State \Return $C_0 \| \cdots \| C_{m-1},\; T$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:aead} gives encryption. Note that the message is always terminated by a padded block, even when $|M|$ is a multiple of 32 or $M$ is empty, whereas an empty $A$ is not absorbed at all; the bit set in $S[15]$ after the associated data separates the two phases. Decryption is the mirror image: each full ciphertext block $C_j$ is XORed with the rate to recover $M_j$ and then \emph{replaces} the rate before $\PP{d}$; for the last block the plaintext is recovered from the truncated keystream and $\mathrm{pad}(M_{m-1})$ is XORed into the rate. The received tag is compared with the recomputed one in constant time; no plaintext is released on failure.

\subsection{\FableStream}
\label{sec:streamspec}
\FableStream is the STREAM construction~\cite{HRRV15} over \FableAEAD. Parameters: a chunk size $L$ (default 65536 bytes, recorded in the header) and a 152-bit file nonce $N_f$. The file header is
\[
H = \texttt{"FBLS"} \,\|\, \texttt{0x01} \,\|\, \mathrm{LE}_{32}(L) \,\|\, N_f \qquad (28 \text{ bytes}),
\]
and the plaintext is split into $n = \lfloor |M|/L \rfloor + 1$ chunks $M_0,\ldots,M_{n-1}$, of which the last has fewer than $L$ bytes and may be empty. Chunk $i$ is encrypted with \FableAEAD under the chunk nonce
\[
N_i = N_f \,\|\, \mathrm{LE}_{32}(i) \,\|\, \mathrm{flag}_i,\qquad \mathrm{flag}_i = \texttt{0x01} \text{ if } i = n-1 \text{ else } \texttt{0x00},
\]
with associated data $H$, producing $C_i \,\|\, T_i$; the output is $H \,\|\, C_0 \| T_0 \| \cdots \| C_{n-1} \| T_{n-1}$. The counter allows $2^{32}$ chunks (256\,TiB at the default chunk size). A final chunk with $\mathrm{flag} = \texttt{0x01}$ is always present, so truncation at a chunk boundary is detected; reordering is detected through the counter and cross-file splicing through $N_f$ and $H$. Decryption verifies chunks in order and stops at the first failure; chunks released before that point are authentic (the online property of STREAM). $N_f$ is either generated uniformly at random or supplied by the caller as a structured value that is unique per key (\S\ref{sec:stream}).

\subsection{\FableHash and \FableXOF}
\label{sec:hashspec}
The hash is a plain sponge with $r = c = 256$ and $\PP{12}$ throughout. The initial state is $\mathrm{IV}_0 \,\|\, \texttt{0x01202000} \,\|\, 0^{14\times 32}$ (the rounds byte of $\mathrm{IV}_1$ is zero, which separates hashing from every AEAD parameter set). Absorption XORs 32-byte blocks into $S[0..7]$ with a $\PP{12}$ after each, the last block $10^*$-padded as above; squeezing outputs $S[0..7]$ and applies $\PP{12}$ between output blocks. \FableHash-256 is the first 32 bytes of the XOF. Claimed: 128-bit security against all attacks (collision, preimage and second preimage), the flat sponge claim for $c = 256$; cf.\ SHAKE128.\footnote{Specification versions v0.1--v0.4 claimed 256-bit preimage resistance for the XOF. That claim was wrong: with $c = 256$ a generic internal-collision (meet-in-the-middle) attack finds preimages in $2^{c/2} = 2^{128}$ work regardless of the output length. Version v0.4.1 and this paper correct it.}

\subsection{Test vectors}
Appendix~\ref{app:vectors} lists $\PP{12}$ and $\PP{8}$ applied to the all-zero state and one AEAD vector per parameter set. The full set (five AEAD vectors per parameter set, two hash vectors, both permutation vectors) is \texttt{test\_vectors\_v0.3.json} in the repository; the \FableF vectors coincide with the v0.1 vectors, which were generated before the parameter set existed.

\section{Design Rationale}
\label{sec:rationale}

Every choice below is tied to an experiment or a proof in \S\ref{sec:security}; the last sentence of each subsection names it.

\subsection{ARX and the \ChaCha double round}
\label{sec:whyarx}
\FableP is an ARX permutation on sixteen 32-bit words for three reasons. First, the operations map one-to-one onto SIMD lanes: a 512-bit state is half an AVX2 register file, eight independent states fit in the eight 32-bit lanes of the sixteen \texttt{ymm} registers, and the rotations by 16 and 8 are byte shuffles. Second, ARX has no table look-ups and no data-dependent branches, so constant-time execution is the default rather than an engineering effort. Third, by taking the round function to be exactly the \ChaCha double round---column step, diagonal step, quarter-round rotations $(16,12,8,7)$---we inherit a body of published cryptanalysis that is unusually deep for a symmetric primitive: differential-linear attacks with probabilistic neutral bits have been refined continuously since 2008~\cite{AFKMR08,BLT20,BBCDFLNT22,CS21,DGSS22,DGM23,WLHL23,Dey24,SDSM25,FT25,FT25b}, and the best of them is the yardstick for our round counts. The price is well known: an ARX round has no provable lower bound on the number of active S-boxes, so no ``$n$ rounds have no characteristic above $2^{-128}$'' statement can be made the way it is for an SPN such as Ascon's S-box layer~\cite{Ascon21}. We accepted this trade because, on CPUs without AES instructions, a wide-trail SPN at the same throughput would need a bitsliced S-box layer, and because the questions an ARX design leaves open are precisely the ones the \ChaCha literature has spent two decades sharpening. The calibration of that literature to \FableP is in \S\ref{sec:dl}, and the exact statements we can make (SAT trail bounds) are in \S\ref{sec:diff} and \S\ref{sec:linear}.

\subsection{Round constants and the column-symmetric subspace}
\label{sec:constants}
\ChaCha is a PRF: its input contains four fixed constant words, and every round acts on a state that the attacker can only influence through the key, nonce and counter positions. A sponge permutation enjoys no such protection---after the first block the whole state is a function of attacker-chosen data---so symmetry must be broken inside the round. Consider the round function $F_0$ with all constants set to zero, i.e.\ the bare \ChaCha double round.

\begin{lemma}[column-symmetric subspace]
Let $\mathcal{V} = \{S : S[4j+k] = S[4j] \text{ for all } j,k \in \{0,1,2,3\}\}$ be the set of states whose four columns are identical ($|\mathcal{V}| = 2^{128}$). Then $F_0(\mathcal{V}) = \mathcal{V}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $S \in \mathcal{V}$ the four column quarter-rounds $Q(S[j],\allowbreak S[4{+}j],\allowbreak S[8{+}j],\allowbreak S[12{+}j])$, $j = 0,\ldots,3$, receive the same input $(S[0],\allowbreak S[4],\allowbreak S[8],\allowbreak S[12])$, hence produce the same output, so the state after the column step is again in $\mathcal{V}$. For such a state the four diagonal quarter-rounds $Q(S[0],S[5],S[10],S[15])$, $Q(S[1],S[6],S[11],S[12])$, $Q(S[2],S[7],S[8],S[13])$, $Q(S[3],S[4],S[9],S[14])$ also receive identical inputs---each diagonal picks one word from each row, and all words of a row are equal---so the diagonal step preserves $\mathcal{V}$ as well.
\end{proof}

The all-zero state is a fixed point of $F_0$ (it lies in $\mathcal{V}$ and $Q(0,0,0,0) = 0$), and $\mathcal{V}$ is the only ``large'' symmetric class we found to be invariant: the classes ``four identical rows'' and ``all sixteen words equal'' are left after one round. A constant XORed into a single word $S[0]$ already makes column 0 differ from columns 1--3; \FableP injects into two words, $S[0]$ and $S[5]$, which lie in different columns (0 and 1), different rows (0 and 1) and on the same diagonal, so that in one round the constant enters two of the four column quarter-rounds and one diagonal quarter-round, and the two injections never cancel: $\mathrm{RC}[i] \oplus (\mathrm{RC}[i] \rotl 16) \neq 0$ for all $i$ and $\mathrm{RC}[i] \neq \mathrm{RC}[j] \rotl 16$ for all $i,j$.

The constants themselves are the fractional parts of $\ln p_i$ for the first twelve primes. We chose the natural logarithm rather than the square or cube roots used by SHA-2 so that no bit pattern is shared with another standard and no ``reused constant'' question arises; the resulting words are pairwise distinct, none is a rotation of another, and their minimum pairwise Hamming distance is 9. A reduced permutation uses the \emph{last} $n$ constants, so that $\PP{8}$ and $\PP{6}$ are suffixes of $\PP{12}$, as in Ascon~\cite{Ascon21}: any analysis of the heavy permutation restricted to its last rounds is directly an analysis of the light one, and the suffix sequence $\mathrm{RC}[6..11]$ does not recur as a window elsewhere in the table, which is what a slide attack between the two permutations would need.

The corresponding checks---the invariance of $\mathcal{V}$ under $F_0$ for 1--12 rounds, its destruction in every one of the twelve rounds on $10^4$ random column-symmetric states, the absence of structured fixed points and of equal or rotated constants---are in \S\ref{sec:constcheck}; the rotational-XOR cost the constants impose is in \S\ref{sec:rx}.

\subsection{Rotation constants}
\label{sec:whyrot}
The rotation amounts $(16,12,8,7)$ are inherited from \ChaCha, and the specification originally marked them provisional pending a screening of all $32^4$ alternatives by differential trail weight. The screening (\S\ref{sec:rotscreen}) found that the natural metrics degenerate in order: the best trail through a single quarter-round has weight 0 for \emph{every} one of the $2^{20}$ choices, because a difference confined to the most significant bit passes all four additions for free (Lemma~\ref{lem:msb}); the best one-round trail of \FableP has weight exactly 2 for every sampled choice without a zero rotation (201,422 of 254,576); and the best 1.5-round trail saturates at weight 12 for 67\,\% of a 2000-choice sample, with no choice above 12. On the only metric that separated candidates---one-round bit-level diffusion---the \ChaCha constants rank in the middle of the top class, and none of the 20 fastest-diffusing candidates beat them on the two-round trail bound (all $\geq 25$, the baseline $\geq \WuncTwo$). Since no differential argument favours a change, and the \ChaCha constants carry the analysis heritage of \S\ref{sec:whyarx}, they were kept; the fact that 16 and 8 are byte shuffles on AVX2 and NEON is a welcome side effect. The full ranking tables are in \S\ref{sec:rotscreen}.

\subsection{Round counts}
\label{sec:whyrounds}
Because no trail-based argument reaches $2^{-128}$ for a full data-phase permutation (the best we prove is a $2^{-\BoundEight}$ split bound for the forgery model of $\PP{8}$, \S\ref{sec:diff}), the round counts are set by an empirical margin: the ratio between the number of \ChaCha single rounds in a permutation and the number reached by the strongest published key-recovery attack on \ChaCha, which is 7.5 as of 2025~\cite{FT25,Dey24,SDSM25}. The default \Fable data phase $\PP{8}$ has 16 single rounds and a margin of $2.1\times$; initialisation and finalisation $\PP{12}$ have 24 and $3.2\times$, more than ChaCha20's own $20/7.5 = 2.7\times$. \FableF keeps $\PP{6}$ (12 single rounds, $1.6\times$) for throughput-bound settings that accept the margin of ChaCha12~\cite{Adiantum18}; it was the only data-phase permutation in versions v0.1--v0.2, and was demoted to an optional set when the $1.6\times$ margin was judged too close to the $1.5\times$ floor the specification sets for itself. A five-round data phase (10 single rounds, $1.33\times$) was considered for speed and removed for the same reason. The comparison is meaningful only if the round constants do not make \FableP's differential-linear behaviour different from \ChaCha's; \S\ref{sec:dl} measures this directly and finds the bias envelopes identical.

\subsection{Rate and capacity}
\label{sec:whyrc}
With $b = 512$, the split $r = c = 256$ gives the keyed duplex its ``beyond $2^{c/2}$'' regime~\cite{JLM14}: the dominant multi-user term is $M N / 2^c$, where $M$ is the total number of processed blocks and $N$ the attacker's offline permutation evaluations~\cite{DMV17}. At $c = 256$ this term is $2^{-37}$ even for $\mu = 2^{32}$ users, $2^{64}$ bytes per user ($M \approx 2^{91}$) and $N = 2^{128}$, and $2^{-69}$ for a single user. A 320-bit rate ($c = 192$) would raise throughput by 25\,\% but makes the same term $2^{27}$ in the multi-user setting and $2^{-5}$ even for one user at the data limit; such a variant could only be offered with a single-user, data-limited claim, so it is not a general-purpose parameter set. The bound and its evaluation are in \S\ref{sec:mu}.

\subsection{The tag is the rate, not the rate XOR the key}
\label{sec:whytag}
\FableAEAD outputs $T = S[0..7]$ after the finalising $\PP{12}$ and does not XOR the key into it. The key has already been injected into the capacity before that permutation, so the tag is the $\tau$-bit truncation of $\PP{12}$ applied to a state that is a function of the key, the nonce, the associated data, the message and the parameter-set identifier. Any two inputs that produce the same tag therefore either produce the same 512-bit state with a fixed key difference (an event of probability at most $q^2/2^{513}$ after $q$ permutation queries) or a collision of a truncated permutation, and the commitment strength is $\tau/2$ bits for the cost of nothing.

The key is injected into the capacity twice more, after initialisation and before finalisation, as in Ascon~\cite{Ascon21}. The first re-injection means that an adversary who recovers the full state during the data phase still does not learn the key; the second means that even a full state recovered after the last data block does not allow forging the tag, since the finalising permutation is applied to a state that depends on the key once again. Both are cheap (eight XORs each) and neither interferes with the commitment argument, because the key is part of the state that the tag commits to either way. XORing the key into the tag, as Ascon does, would not obviously weaken this---the adversary would then need $F(X) \oplus F(X') = K \oplus K'$---but it would make the reduction depend on a relation between the key and the tag rather than on the collision resistance of a public function alone, and we preferred the argument whose only assumption is that $\PP{12}$ has no collision shortcut. The reduction is Theorem~\ref{thm:cmt} in \S\ref{sec:commitment}.

\subsection{Nonce length, file nonce, chunk size and STREAM}
\label{sec:whystream}
The AEAD nonce is 192 bits so that \FableStream can spend 32 bits on a chunk counter and 8 bits on the end-of-file flag and still leave 152 random bits for the file nonce $N_f$. For $F$ files under one key the probability that two random file nonces coincide is at most $F^2/2^{153}$: $2^{-73}$ at $F = 2^{40}$ and $2^{-57}$ in the degenerate case of $2^{48}$ single-chunk files that exhausts the $2^{64}$-byte budget. This is the largest term in the concrete security bound (\S\ref{sec:mu}), which is why the specification also admits a caller-supplied structured $N_f$ with zero collision term. The default chunk of 64\,KiB is a compromise between overhead and granularity: the 32-byte tag per chunk is 0.05\,\% of the data, the two $\PP{12}$ calls per chunk add about 0.2\,\% to the round count of the 2048 data-phase permutations, and 64\,KiB is large enough for the eight-lane AVX2 implementation to amortise the transposition of state and data. STREAM~\cite{HRRV15} was chosen over a single long duplex stream because it gives, at that cost, independent chunks (thread- and lane-level parallelism, random access on decryption---with the truncation guarantee requiring the final chunk to be checked), online decryption in which every released chunk is already authenticated, and a tight reduction to the nonce-based AEAD security of \FableAEAD that covers truncation, reordering and cross-file splicing. The STREAM reduction and the nonce term are in \S\ref{sec:mu} and \S\ref{sec:stream}; the throughput of the eight-lane implementation is in \S\ref{sec:impl}.

\section{Security Analysis}
\label{sec:security}

All results in this section are first-party and reproducible: every number is taken from the analysis log of the repository (\repo), which records for each the command that regenerates it and the raw data file it was computed from; we cite those files inline. Three distinctions are kept strictly throughout. A \emph{trail} (characteristic) fixes the difference or mask at every intermediate step and its probability or correlation is computed under the usual independence assumption; a \emph{differential} (resp.\ linear hull) aggregates all trails with the same input and output and can only have larger probability (resp.\ a different correlation). A statement is \emph{exact} when the SAT solver has both found a trail of weight $W$ and proved that none of weight $W-1$ exists; it is a \emph{SAT lower bound} on the weight (hence an upper bound on the trail probability) when the solver proved ``no trail of weight $\leq W-1$'' and was stopped before finding one; it is a \emph{split bound} when it is the sum of bounds for consecutive segments (\S\ref{sec:split}). Bounds on weights are lower bounds; the corresponding bounds on probabilities are upper bounds.

\subsection{Avalanche and diffusion}
\label{sec:avalanche}
\paragraph{Model.} For $n$ rounds of \FableP, each of the 512 input bits is flipped in $N = 5\times10^5$ uniformly random states and, for each of the $512\times512$ (input bit, output bit) pairs, the fraction $p$ of states in which the output bit changed is recorded (\texttt{c/avalanche.c}, data \texttt{data/avalanche/av\_r\{1..6\}.u32}). Under the null hypothesis $p = 1/2$ the estimator has standard deviation $\sigma = 0.5/\sqrt{N} = 7.1\times10^{-4}$, and the expected maximum of $2^{18}$ independent Gaussian deviations is about $\sigma\sqrt{2\ln 2^{18}} \approx 5\sigma \approx 0.0035$. A structural bias of $0.005$ would therefore stand out at $7\sigma$, twice the noise ceiling.

\subsection{Differential trails}
\label{sec:diff}
\paragraph{Model.} XOR differences propagate through XOR and rotation deterministically and through modular addition with the Lipmaa--Moriai conditions~\cite{LM01}: $(\alpha,\beta\to\gamma)$ is a valid differential of $z = x + y$ iff $\alpha_0\oplus\beta_0\oplus\gamma_0 = 0$ and, for $0 \le i \le 30$, $\alpha_i=\beta_i=\gamma_i$ implies $\alpha_{i+1}\oplus\beta_{i+1}\oplus\gamma_{i+1} = \alpha_i$; its weight is the number of positions $i \le 30$ with $\neg(\alpha_i=\beta_i=\gamma_i)$ and its probability $2^{-\text{weight}}$. Round constants are transparent to XOR differences. The permutation is encoded in CNF with one weight literal per addition bit (Appendix~\ref{app:sat}), the total weight is bounded with an incremental totalizer, and the minimum is found by increasing the bound until the instance is satisfiable (CaDiCaL~\cite{CaDiCaL} through PySAT~\cite{PySAT18}); this is the approach Mouha and Preneel applied to Salsa20~\cite{MP13}, with a SAT rather than an SMT back end. Found trails are checked empirically by sampling through the C permutation (\texttt{sat/verify\_trail.py}).

\begin{lemma}[probability-one quarter-round differential]
\label{lem:msb}
For every choice of rotation constants $(r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3)$, the quarter-round $Q$ has a differential of probability one with a nonzero input difference, namely
$(\Delta a,\Delta b,\Delta c,\Delta d) = (0,\ \mathrm{MSB},\ 0,\ \mathrm{MSB}\oplus \mathrm{bit}(31-r_0)) \to (\mathrm{MSB},\ \mathrm{bit}(r_3-1),\ \mathrm{MSB},\ 0)$, where $\mathrm{MSB} = \mathrm{bit}(31)$ and $\mathrm{bit}(j) = 2^j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A difference confined to the most significant bit passes through a modular addition with probability one, because no carry propagates out of bit 31. In $a \mathrel{+}= b$ the difference $\mathrm{MSB}$ of $b$ becomes $\Delta a = \mathrm{MSB}$. In $d \mathrel{\oplus}= a$ the MSB of $\Delta d$ cancels and $\mathrm{bit}(31-r_0)$ remains, which $d \rotl r_0$ moves to the MSB. Then $c \mathrel{+}= d$ gives $\Delta c = \mathrm{MSB}$ with probability one, $b \mathrel{\oplus}= c$ cancels $\Delta b$ to zero, so $b \rotl r_1$ carries no difference; $a \mathrel{+}= b$ leaves $\Delta a = \mathrm{MSB}$; $d \mathrel{\oplus}= a$ cancels $\Delta d$ to zero; $c \mathrel{+}= d$ leaves $\Delta c = \mathrm{MSB}$; finally $b \mathrel{\oplus}= c$ sets $\Delta b = \mathrm{MSB}$ and $b \rotl r_3$ moves it to $\mathrm{bit}(r_3-1)$. No addition ever sees a difference below bit 31, so the total weight is 0.
\end{proof}
For $(16,12,8,7)$ the SAT search returns exactly this trail and $10^5$ random samples confirm it; the search over all $32^4$ rotation choices (\S\ref{sec:rotscreen}) returns weight 0 for each, as the lemma predicts. This is the known behaviour of the \ChaCha quarter-round, inherited unchanged.

\subsection{Linear trails and hulls}
\label{sec:linear}
\paragraph{Model.} A linear approximation $(u,v\to w)$ of $z = x+y$ is modelled exactly through the carry chain (Wall\'en~\cite{Wallen03}, Schulte-Geers~\cite{SchulteGeers13}): with a mask $g_i$ on every carry bit, the local approximation at bit $i$ has correlation $1$ if $g_{i+1} = 0$ and $u_i\oplus w_i = v_i\oplus w_i = g_i\oplus w_i = 0$, correlation $\pm\tfrac12$ if $g_{i+1} = 1$ and $(u_i\oplus w_i)\oplus(v_i\oplus w_i)\oplus(g_i\oplus w_i) = 1$, and $0$ otherwise ($g_0$ absent, $g_{32} = 0$). Since each state has at most one successor, the carry-mask path is unique and $|\mathrm{corr}| = 2^{-\#\{i: g_i = 1\}}$ exactly; XOR duplicates the output mask onto both inputs, a value used twice receives the XOR of the two masks, and round constants only flip signs (Appendix~\ref{app:sat}). The weight $W$ of a trail gives correlation $2^{-W}$ (bias $2^{-W-1}$).

\paragraph{Results.} The quarter-round has a correlation-one linear relation, the LSB relation $z_0 = x_0\oplus y_0$ carried through four additions (input masks $(\mathrm{bit}(0),0,0,\mathrm{bit}(0))$; measured correlation $+1.0000$). One round of \FableP has an exact optimum of weight 1 (input mask $S[0].b_0 \oplus S[4].b_0$, one half-correlation at bit 1 of one addition); its measured correlation is $-0.5017$ over $2\times10^5$ samples, the sign coming from the constants. With input and output masks confined to the rate (the keystream-distinguishing model) one round has weight $\geq 28$ (SAT lower bound, stopped); two rounds unconstrained have weight $\geq \WlinTwo$ (SAT lower bound, stopped, being raised in the background); no two-round trail of weight $\leq 24$ is known. Data: \texttt{data/lin/}.

\paragraph{Hulls.} For a fixed input/output mask pair we enumerate \emph{all} trails up to a weight cap and sum their signed correlations (\texttt{sat/lin\_hull.py}), computing each sign from the Walsh coefficients of the carry function (a single variable $+\tfrac12$, three variables $-\tfrac12$; at bit 0 the AND with $x\oplus y$ is $-\tfrac12$) and from the constants. For the one-round optimum only one trail exists up to weight 6: no hull effect, and the enumeration reproduces the measured $-0.5$ including its sign. For two rounds we took a trail of weight 48 obtained by fixing the input mask to the one-round optimum and enumerated around it (Table~\ref{tab:hull}): the hull correlation converges to $2^{-44.78}$, a factor $9.3 \approx 2^{3.2}$ above the best single trail, by a cap of 58.

\subsection{Rotational-XOR}
\label{sec:rx}
\paragraph{Model.} A rotational pair $(x, x\rotl k)$ survives a modular addition with probability $p_k = (1 + 2^{k-32} + 2^{-k} + 2^{-32})/4$~\cite{KN10}: $2^{-1.415}$ for $k = 1$, $2^{-2.0}$ for $k = 16$. This is paid at every one of the 32 additions per round regardless of constants: $2^{-45.3}$ per round, $2^{-271.7}$ for $\PP{6}$ and $2^{-543.4}$ for $\PP{12}$ at $k = 1$. A constant $c$ XORed into a word changes the rotational-XOR difference by $c \oplus (c\rotl k)$~\cite{AL16}, which must then pass the following additions at a cost of roughly its Hamming weight minus at most 2.

\paragraph{Result.} For $k = 1$ the injected weights per round are $\mathrm{hw}(\mathrm{RC}[i]\oplus(\mathrm{RC}[i]\rotl 1)) = 14, 18, 10, 16, 16, 18, 16, 10, 16, 14, 16, 14$ for $i = 0..11$, and the same values for the $S[5]$ injection; no round injects a zero difference for any $k \in \{1,\ldots,31\}$ (minimum $\mathrm{hw}(d_0)+\mathrm{hw}(d_5) = 16$, at $k = 4$, round 8). Adding the crude lower bound $\max(0,\mathrm{hw}-2)$ per injection gives $\geq 148$ extra bits for $\PP{6}$ and $\geq 308$ for $\PP{12}$, i.e.\ rotational-XOR trail probabilities of at most $2^{-419.7}$ for $\PP{6}$ and $2^{-851.4}$ for $\PP{12}$ at $k = 1$ (lower still for other $k$). Empirically, one quarter-round is rotational with probability $2^{-7.18}$ over $2^{20}$ samples without a constant (the independent-addition model predicts $2^{-5.66}$; chained additions are known to make the true value smaller~\cite{KNPSS15}), and $0$ of $2^{20}$ after XORing $\mathrm{RC}[6]$ into $a$. The structure alone puts rotational attacks far below $2^{-256}$ for six rounds; the constants add to that, and an exact SAT model of rotational-XOR trails with constants was therefore not built. Data: \texttt{data/rx\_check.txt}.

\subsection{Constants, symmetry, slide and fixed points}
\label{sec:constcheck}
The claims of \S\ref{sec:constants} were checked by \path{tools/check_constants.py} (output \path{data/constants_check.txt}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item the twelve constants recomputed from $\ln p_i$ with 60-digit precision match the table; they are pairwise distinct, none equals another rotated by any amount, no $\mathrm{RC}[i]$ equals $\mathrm{RC}[j]\rotl 16$, the minimum pairwise Hamming distance is 9, and $\mathrm{RC}[i]\oplus(\mathrm{RC}[i]\rotl 16) \neq 0$ for all $i$;
\item with constants set to zero, the all-zero state is a fixed point of 12 rounds and the column-symmetric subspace $\mathcal{V}$ is preserved by 1--12 rounds, whereas the row-symmetric and all-equal classes are left after one round;
\item with the constants, every structured input (all-zero, all-one, all-$\mathrm{MSB}$, all-1, three random members each of $\mathcal{V}$, of the row-symmetric class and of the all-equal class) gives an output after $n = 1..12$ rounds---counted both as a prefix and as a suffix of $\PP{12}$---that is neither symmetric nor a fixed point, and $10^4$ random members of $\mathcal{V}$ leave $\mathcal{V}$ after every one of the twelve rounds;
\item slide: no two constants are equal, no constant is a rotation of another, the suffix $\mathrm{RC}[6..11]$ used by $\PP{6}$ is not periodic and does not occur as another window of the table, and the eleven consecutive XOR differences $\mathrm{RC}[i]\oplus\mathrm{RC}[i+1]$ are distinct (no affine slide);
\item fixed points: none of the structured inputs is a fixed point of any $\PP{n}$, and $2^{18}$ random states contain no one-round fixed point. A random permutation has about one fixed point; only structured ones can be excluded.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Key commitment}
\label{sec:commitment}
Let $X = (Y_R, Y_C \oplus K)$ be the state just before the finalising permutation, where $(Y_R, Y_C)$ is the state after the last (padded) message block has been XORed into the rate; $T = \mathrm{trunc}_\tau(\PP{12}(X)[0..7])$. $X$ is a deterministic function of $(K, N, A, M)$ and of the parameter set (through $\mathrm{IV}_1$), and for a fixed $(C, T)$ and $(K', N', A')$ the message $M'$ is determined by decryption, so CMT-1 (two keys) and CMT-4 (two distinct tuples) in the sense of Bellare and Hoang~\cite{BH22} both amount to producing two tuples with the same $(C,T)$.

\begin{theorem}[key commitment]
\label{thm:cmt}
Model $\PP{12}$ as a random permutation $\pi$ of $\{0,1\}^{512}$ to which the adversary makes $q$ forward or inverse queries. Any adversary that outputs two distinct tuples $(K,N,A,M) \neq (K',N',A',M')$ encrypting to the same $(C,T)$ under \FableAEAD with a $\tau$-bit tag succeeds with probability at most
\[
\frac{q(q-1)}{2^{\tau+1}} + \frac{q(q-1)}{2^{513}} + \frac{q}{2^{256}}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Let $X, X'$ be the two pre-finalisation states. (i) If $X \neq X'$, then $\mathrm{trunc}_\tau(\pi(X)) = \allowbreak \mathrm{trunc}_\tau(\pi(X'))$: a collision of the $\tau$-bit truncation of $\pi$ on two distinct queried inputs, probability at most $\binom{q}{2}/2^\tau$ up to the negligible bias of a truncated permutation. (ii) If $X = X'$ and $K \neq K'$, then $Y_R = Y'_R$ and $Y_C \oplus Y'_C = K\oplus K'$: two duplex paths through $\pi$, ending in different queries, whose final states satisfy a fixed 512-bit relation, probability at most $\binom{q}{2}/2^{512}$. (If $K = K'$ and the tuples differ elsewhere, $X = X'$ is a full internal state collision, covered by the same count.) (iii) Otherwise the adversary has guessed the 256-bit capacity of some path, probability at most $q/2^{256}$. A successful output is witnessed by one of the three events, and values of $\pi$ that were never queried are uniform and cannot produce an equal tag except by the same counting.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
With $\tau = 256$ the bound is $\approx q^2/2^{257}$: about $2^{128}$ permutation evaluations. With $\tau = 128$ it is $\approx q^2/2^{129}$: about $2^{64}$. Both are tight, since a generic birthday search on $\mathrm{trunc}_\tau\circ\PP{12}$ achieves them. Commitment is an offline collision problem and is not affected by the number of users or by nonce reuse. The specification's claims ``128-bit commitment with 256-bit tags, 64-bit with 128-bit tags'' are therefore correct in the ideal-permutation model.
\end{corollary}
The model's practical premise is that $\PP{12}$ admits no collision shortcut, i.e.\ no 12-round differential with zero rate-output difference of probability above about $2^{-128}$ (the cost of the generic birthday attack); the split of the two-round bound gives trail probabilities $\leq 2^{-\the\numexpr 6*\WuncTwo\relax}$ for twelve rounds ($6\times \WuncTwo$), and 24 single rounds are beyond every published \ChaCha differential or collision-type result (\S\ref{sec:dl}). The design choice behind the theorem is discussed in \S\ref{sec:whytag}; the argument is written out in \texttt{docs/key-commitment.md}.

\subsection{Multi-user and STREAM bounds}
\label{sec:mu}
\paragraph{Model.} \FableAEAD is a keyed duplex with $b = 512$, $r = c = 256$, $k = 256$; $\mu$ users hold independent keys; the adversary makes $N$ offline permutation evaluations, $M$ online data blocks in total (32 bytes each, plus two calls per message for initialisation and finalisation) and $q_D$ verification queries; nonces are respected. \subsection{Fable-Stream nonce}
\label{sec:stream}
Within one file the chunk nonces $N_f\,\|\,\mathrm{LE}_{32}(i)\,\|\,\mathrm{flag}_i$ are distinct by construction for up to $2^{32}$ chunks. Across files under one key the 152-bit $N_f$ must be unique; when it is drawn at random this holds except with probability $F^2/2^{153}$ ($[F]$ above), and when it is structured (for instance a 64-bit device or session identifier followed by an 88-bit counter maintained by the caller) it holds unconditionally. Nonce reuse is not tolerated: as for any nonce-based scheme without misuse resistance, a repeated $(K, N_i)$ pair reveals the XOR of the two chunk plaintexts and the keystream of that position, and lets the adversary forge for that nonce.

\subsection{What is not covered}
\label{sec:notcovered}
The following are not established by this paper and are listed so that they are not mistaken for results:
\begin{itemize}
\item direct (non-split) differential trail bounds for three or more rounds; the three-round rate-restricted bound of $\geq 43$ is the furthest the solver reached within its time cap;
\item exact optima for two rounds, both differential ($\geq \WuncTwo$ unconstrained, $\geq \WrateTwo$ rate-restricted) and linear ($\geq \WlinTwo$); the searches were stopped, not completed;
\item differential (clustering) probabilities as opposed to trail probabilities; the $\the\numexpr\BoundEight-128\relax$-bit margin of the $2^{-\BoundEight}$ split bound over $2^{-128}$ does not cover clustering, and the corresponding hull allowance for linear cryptanalysis was measured only for one two-round mask pair;
\item algebraic, cube and integral attacks, and guess-and-determine attacks on the initialisation;
\item fault, probing and other physical attacks (excluded by the specification);
\item dedicated analysis of \FableHash beyond the flat sponge claim inherited from the permutation;
\item any third-party cryptanalysis.
\end{itemize}

\section{Implementation and Performance}
\label{sec:impl}

\subsection{Implementations}
\label{sec:impls}
Three implementations exist in the repository. The Python reference (\texttt{fable.py}) is the normative one and is deliberately slow and literal. The portable C implementation (\texttt{c/fable\_p.c}, \texttt{c/fable\_aead.c}) is scalar and word-oriented, with the rotation amounts as compile-time constants so that the compiler emits immediate rotates. The AVX2 implementation (\texttt{c/fable\_p\_avx2.c}, \texttt{c/fable\_stream\_avx2.c}) processes eight \FableStream chunks at once: each of the sixteen \texttt{ymm} registers holds the same state word of eight independent states (lane $=$ chunk), so the quarter-round is executed with \texttt{vpaddd}/\texttt{vpxor}, the rotations by 16 and 8 as byte shuffles (\texttt{vpshufb}) and those by 12 and 7 as shift-or pairs. The eight states stay in this transposed layout for the whole AEAD---initialisation ($\PP{12}$), the header block, every data block ($\PP{d}$) and finalisation ($\PP{12}$)---and data enters and leaves through an $8\times8$ transpose of 32-bit words (three \texttt{unpack}/\texttt{permute2x128} stages), twice per 32-byte block. Chunk groups of fewer than eight and the final chunk take the scalar path. Decryption mirrors encryption: the transposed ciphertext block is XORed with the rate to give the plaintext and then \emph{replaces} the rate; the last partial block is recovered from the keystream and its padded plaintext XORed back; the eight tags are recomputed and compared lane by lane in constant time, and a failure in any lane zeroes the whole output and returns an error.

Cross-validation covers, for both parameter sets: the permutation vectors $\PP{12}(0)$ and $\PP{8}(0)$; the ten AEAD and two hash vectors of \texttt{test\_vectors\_v0.3.json} (the \FableF vectors equal the v0.1 vectors); 50 random states for every round count 1--12 and 200 random rotation-constant sets against the Python permutation; 36 random (message length, AD length) combinations per set against Python encryption and C decryption; nine chunk-size/length combinations of \FableStream per set (chunk sizes 32, 1000, 4096, 65536 bytes; zero to three AVX2 groups; empty and partial final chunks; chunk sizes that are not multiples of 32) with AVX2 and scalar encryption equal to Python, AVX2 and scalar decryption returning the plaintext, Python decrypting the C output, and single-bit tampering in a middle chunk, tampering of the final tag, truncation and decryption under the wrong parameter set all rejected; and the AVX2 permutation against the scalar one on 2000 random states $\times$ 8 lanes for every round count 1--12 (\texttt{tests/check\_c\_vectors.py}, \texttt{tests/check\_stream\_avx2.py}, \texttt{c/test\_avx2.c}).

\subsection{Constant-time properties}
\label{sec:ct}
By construction the implementations contain no table look-ups and no branches on secret data: the permutation is straight-line ARX code, block handling depends only on lengths, the tag comparison accumulates the OR of byte differences and tests it once, and the AVX2 lanes do not interact except through the data-independent transposes. We state this as a property of the code as written, not as a measured result: no dudect- or ctgrind-style verification, and no measurement on a real target, has been carried out (\S\ref{sec:notmeasured}).

\subsection{Measurement method}
\label{sec:method}
All measurements were taken on one machine: an Intel Raptor Lake desktop processor (8 performance cores with hyper-threading and 8 efficiency cores, 24 logical CPUs) under Windows 11, code compiled with clang 21.1.0 at \texttt{-O3 -mavx2}. Cycle counts use \texttt{rdtsc}; because the TSC runs at a fixed reference frequency, the actual core clock is calibrated in the same run by timing a chain of $4\times10^8$ dependent one-cycle additions, and cycles per byte (cpb) are reported in core cycles. The benchmark process runs at high priority with its main thread pinned to logical CPU 0 (a performance core) and, for multi-threaded runs, each worker pinned to a distinct performance core; every figure is the best of three (permutation micro-benchmarks: five) repetitions. Two caveats apply. First, background SAT searches were running at low priority during all measurements, and the calibrated core clock varied between runs (about 3.0\,GHz under heavy load for the permutation micro-benchmarks, 3.65\,GHz for the first \FableStream measurement, 4.89\,GHz for the final one); absolute GB/s figures therefore differ between runs while cpb figures agree to within a few percent, and cpb is the number to compare. Second, an early run that left the main thread unpinned was scheduled on an efficiency core and reported roughly half the throughput; all figures below are from pinned runs.

\section{Conclusion and Open Problems}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\Fable puts one new object on the table---a 512-bit ARX permutation that is the \ChaCha double round with round constants---and builds everything else from constructions with existing proofs: a keyed duplex whose tag is the truncated output of the finalising permutation and therefore commits to the key, the nonce, the associated data and the message at no extra cost; a STREAM mode whose chunks are independent enough to fill eight AVX2 lanes and to be decrypted online; and a sponge hash. The first-party analysis gives exact one-round trail optima, SAT lower bounds for two rounds, a split bound of $2^{-\BoundEight}$ on any forgery trail through the default data phase, a differential-linear calibration showing that the constants do not alter the \ChaCha bias envelope (so that the $2.1\times$ and $3.2\times$ round-count margins can be read against the \ChaCha literature), a hull enumeration, rotational-XOR, symmetry and slide checks, and mode-level bounds; the AVX2 implementation runs at 2.00\,GB/s single-threaded, on par with OpenSSL's ChaCha20-Poly1305 on the same machine, and 2.53\,GB/s for \FableF.

\paragraph{Open problems}, in the order in which we think they matter:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Two-round optima and clustering.} The $2^{-\BoundEight}$ bound rests on two SAT lower bounds ($\WrateTwo$ rate-restricted, $\WuncTwo$ unconstrained) that were stopped, not completed, and it bounds trails, not differentials. Finding the exact two-round optima, and estimating how many trails share the input/output differences of the best ones, would say whether the $\the\numexpr\BoundEight-128\relax$-bit margin over $2^{-128}$ is real. Every bit gained on both two-round bounds adds four bits to the $\PP{8}$ bound.
\item \emph{A dedicated attack on the data phase.} We calibrated \FableP against \ChaCha but did not attempt a differential-linear or PNB-style attack in the duplex model itself, where the adversary controls a 256-bit rate difference, sees only 256 output bits and faces a 256-bit secret capacity. Whether that model is easier or harder than the \ChaCha PRF model for 16 single rounds is the question the round count depends on.
\item \emph{Algebraic and cube attacks on initialisation.} The initialisation permutation has a 64-bit constant prefix, a 256-bit key and a 192-bit chosen nonce; cube and conditional-cube attacks on 24 single rounds have not been evaluated.
\item \emph{\FableHash.} Only the flat sponge claim inherited from the permutation is made; collision and preimage attacks on reduced rounds of the hash have not been studied.
\item \emph{NEON and short messages.} No ARM implementation exists, and the latency of a single message shorter than a chunk, where the two $\PP{12}$ calls dominate, has not been measured.
\item \emph{Side channels.} The constant-time properties are argued from the code and have not been tested on hardware.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Status.} All analysis reported here is first-party; no third party has examined \Fable, and the differential bounds beyond two rounds are split bounds rather than direct ones. \Fable must not be used to protect real data until independent cryptanalysis has been published. The specification, the reference and C/AVX2 implementations, every script and every raw data file behind the numbers in this paper, and the analysis log that records what was tried and what failed, are available at \texttt{<repository URL>}; a preprint is at \texttt{<ePrint URL>}. We invite analysis of the permutation, of the rate-restricted duplex model, and of the claims in \S\ref{sec:security}.

\section{SAT models}
\label{app:sat}
Both models are in \texttt{sat/} of the repository (\texttt{fable\_diff.py}, \texttt{fable\_lin.py}, \texttt{lin\_hull.py}) and use PySAT~\cite{PySAT18} with CaDiCaL 1.5.3~\cite{CaDiCaL} (Glucose~4 only where interruptible solving was needed).

\paragraph{Differential model.} Every 32-bit difference on a wire is a vector of 32 Boolean variables. XOR $z = x\oplus y$ introduces a fresh word $z$ with four clauses per bit; a rotation is a re-indexing. For $z = x + y$ with differences $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ we add, for $i = 0..30$, a weight variable $w_i$ with the clauses $\neg w_i \Rightarrow (\alpha_i = \beta_i = \gamma_i)$, $w_i \Rightarrow \neg(\alpha_i = \beta_i = \gamma_i)$ and $\neg w_i \Rightarrow (\alpha_{i+1}\oplus\beta_{i+1}\oplus\gamma_{i+1}\oplus\alpha_i = 0)$ (14 clauses per bit), plus $\alpha_0\oplus\beta_0\oplus\gamma_0 = 0$; this is the Lipmaa--Moriai characterisation~\cite{LM01}, the trail weight is $\sum w_i$, and the quarter-round is the sequence of four additions, four XORs and four rotations of \S\ref{sec:perm}. One round is 32 additions ($992$ weight variables, $3552$ variables, $18\,112$ clauses); two rounds double this. A clause requires the input difference to be nonzero. The rate-restricted variants add unit clauses fixing the capacity part of the input (\textsf{rate-in}) or output (\textsf{cap-out-zero}) difference to zero, or a clause requiring a nonzero rate output difference. The total weight is bounded by an incremental totalizer (PySAT \texttt{ITotalizer}); the search asks ``weight $\leq k$?'' for $k = k_0, k_0+1, \ldots$ and stops at the first satisfiable $k$ (exact optimum) or at a time cap (lower bound $k+1$). Found trails are decoded to hexadecimal differences at every round boundary and verified by sampling random pairs through the C permutation.

\paragraph{Linear model.} Masks are again 32 Boolean variables per wire. For an addition with masks $(u,v,w)$ we introduce carry-mask variables $g_1,\ldots,g_{31}$ and encode, for $i = 1..31$: $g_{i+1} = 0 \Rightarrow u_i = v_i = g_i = w_i$ and $g_{i+1} = 1 \Rightarrow u_i\oplus v_i\oplus g_i\oplus w_i = 1$ (with $g_{32} = 0$ forcing $u_{31} = v_{31} = g_{31} = w_{31}$), and at bit 0: $g_1 = 0 \Rightarrow u_0 = v_0 = w_0$. The weight is $\sum_{i=1}^{31} g_i$ and the trail correlation $2^{-\text{weight}}$ exactly (\S\ref{sec:linear}). XOR gates copy the output mask to both inputs; a wire used twice carries the XOR of the two consumers' masks, which is realised by a fresh word and four clauses per bit. The quarter-round therefore introduces, per addition, the masks on its two inputs and its output plus the fork masks. The hull enumerator fixes the input and output masks of a given trail, bounds the weight with the totalizer, and after every satisfying assignment adds a blocking clause on all wire-mask variables; the sign of each trail is the product of the local Walsh signs ($-\tfrac12$ when the three-variable majority approximation is used, and at bit 0 when the AND is approximated by $x\oplus y$) and of $(-1)^{\langle m, c\rangle}$ for every constant $c$ XORed into a wire carrying mask $m$.
